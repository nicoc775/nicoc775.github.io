Pillars of Creation (Eagle Nebula)

The Pillars of Creation are towering columns of gas and dust located inside the Eagle Nebula in the constellation Serpens. They are one of the most famous images captured by the Hubble Space Telescope and show an active region where new stars are being formed.

These giant structures are made of cold hydrogen gas and dust. Inside them, dense regions collapse under gravity, forming new stars. Strong ultraviolet radiation from nearby young stars slowly erodes the pillars over time.

The pillars are part of a much larger star-forming region about 7,000 light-years from Earth. They are constantly shaped by stellar winds and radiation, creating their iconic finger-like appearance.
